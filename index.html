Why we made this

Every December, with great anticipation, our apps tell us what we did with our year. Spotify counts our minutes and gives us an age. Letterboxd counts our films and tells us our patterns. It’s so fun, but it’s also a little odd, because counts are the least interesting thing about a year!

Nobody remembers an album as 2,340 minutes over the last 365 days. You remember it as the walk home after Ship & Shovell, the soundtrack to working from home, or the friend who sent it on Slack on a Friday afternoon with no message attached.

How many times you rewatched a film isn’t the point. What it did to you is. That’s why our film and book clubs exist: for the chance of passing on what it did to us.

The Argentine writer Borges, borrowing from the philosopher Berkeley, said something similar long before any of this:

“the taste of the apple lies in the contact of the fruit with the palate, not in the fruit itself.”

So this isn’t a list of things we’ve simply seen. It’s a list of things we carry forward. Calvino said a classic is a book that has never finished saying what it has to say, and most of us have at least one of those (book, film, exhibition, album) from this year, even if we haven’t had the chance to stop and think about it.

In a year when machines can make something that looks a lot like art, the meaning is still made at our end: in choosing, feeling, and passing it on.

So that’s PUBLIC Wrapped: what moved us, made us think, or is staying with us for at least another year. Have a look at what everyone else is taking with them!
